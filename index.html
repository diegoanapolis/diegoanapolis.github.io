About

I have been a forensic expert (perito criminal) at the Civil Police of the Federal District since 2018 and hold a PhD in Chemistry from the Federal University of Goiás. At the Criminalistics Institute, I lead applied research lines that start from a recurring analytical problem: identifying and quantifying substances in mixtures quickly, at low cost and with little waste.

This page presents those lines, the results published so far, and the students and collaborators who take part in them.

Institutional basis for research

The Civil Police of the Federal District (PCDF) is a public government institution, and the Criminalistics Institute (IC) is one of the forensic institutes of its Technical Police Department. Scientific research is a formal duty of the institution and of the position of Perito Criminal. The PCDF Internal Regulations list organizing and conducting technical and scientific research among the institution's responsibilities (Art. 4, XVII). They assign the Criminalistics Institute the task of fostering scientific studies and working with teaching and research institutions (Art. 170, X). And they include planning, developing and carrying out scientific research and studies among the duties of the Perito Criminal (Art. 204, IX). In 2011, the PCDF signed a technical cooperation protocol with CNPq, Brazil's National Council for Scientific and Technological Development, for carrying out scientific, technological and innovation research projects. The published extract sets an indefinite term.

Current research

Two ongoing lines assess how far frontier generative models can be trusted to interpret forensic analytical data, always compared against a reference method. I coordinate both and am responsible for the study design, the data analysis and the validation. FTIR screening is the main project; the GC-MS study complements it.

FTIR screening of seized powders

Can cocaine be detected in white powders, which are almost always mixtures, using only the infrared (FTIR) spectrum?

FTIR is fast, inexpensive and solvent-free. The difficulty is that adulterants and diluents overlap the cocaine bands. This line compares two routes:

  • chemometric and machine-learning models, including spectral unmixing, which decomposes the spectrum of a mixture into the contributions of pure-substance spectra, with large-scale testing of preprocessing methods and models, run with AI coding agents;
  • direct interpretation of the spectrum by frontier generative models, which receive the chemical context of the bands and of their splitting.

In the validation set, every sample will have a reference result by gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS).

Stage: preliminary results with simulated mixtures, built by combining spectra of pure substances. In preparation: the validation set, with paired FTIR and GC-MS data from real and laboratory-prepared samples.

Published groundwork: Scott test with multivariate image analysis (Forensic Science International, 2022); new cocaine cutting agents, Irgafos 168 and Irganox 1076 (Forensic Science International, 2024); infrared with multivariate techniques and artificial intelligence (Química Nova, 2012).

Interpretation of GC-MS data by generative models

Can frontier generative models read chromatograms and mass spectra with the reliability that chemical analysis requires?

This line evaluates three tasks:

  1. identifying substances from the chromatogram and the mass spectrum;
  2. checking the findings against a dated version of the lists of controlled substances (in Brazil, Ordinance SVS/MS No. 344/1998) and against known adulterants;
  3. for unknown substances, supporting and speeding up interpretation, with indications of polarity and volatility from chromatographic retention and structural hypotheses from the mass spectrum, to guide and complement the elucidation of new chemical structures.

Model answers are compared with spectral library search and with the expert's interpretation.

Stage: exploratory tests under way.

How the research is conducted

  • The studies use instrumental data, such as spectra and chromatograms, in a research setting, with no information that identifies people or proceedings.
  • A model's output is treated as a hypothesis: every answer is reviewed and verified by a forensic expert.
  • GC-MS, interpreted by the forensic expert, is the reference result. Models are evaluated on separate test samples, and the reference results are used only for the comparison.
  • Each study reports performance indicators: accuracy, false positives, false negatives and how often wrong answers are given with apparent confidence.
  • Methods, code and non-sensitive data will be published openly.

Generative AI in practice: AlcoLab and open science

AlcoLab is the first result of this way of working: a new screening method, developed with the support of generative AI tools from Anthropic (Claude Code and Claude Cowork) and OpenAI (Codex), and published as an open-access article with open code and data.

What it is. A reagent-free method for semi-quantitative screening of methanol in distilled beverages and commercial ethanol solutions. It combines two simple measurements: relative density, on an ordinary balance, and flow time through a disposable 20 mL syringe with a needle. An application that runs in the browser, including offline, interprets the measurements and returns the result as a traffic-light signal.

What it is for. Flagging suspect samples and prioritizing confirmatory analysis. It is a proof of concept for screening and does not replace chromatography.

Why it matters. Outbreaks of methanol poisoning from adulterated beverages are recurrent, as in the 2025 crisis in Brazil, and reference methods are rarely available in the field.

How AI was used. The study design, the analytical methodology, the software requirements and the code architecture were defined by the authors. Within that design, these tools supported the implementation of the analysis scripts, the figures and the application code, as well as the language review of the manuscript. All AI-assisted code and text was reviewed, tested and validated by the authors, as the article itself states.

Independent initiative. AlcoLab was developed with collaborators from the Federal University of Maranhão and is not an IC-PCDF project. The experimental work was carried out without dedicated funding; FAPDF, the Federal District Research Support Foundation, supports the project and its publication through the Deep Tech program. The application is free and open source (AGPL-3.0 license). After the study, the company Baru Tech was created and is now responsible for maintaining the application.

Co-authors: Pedro Augusto de Oliveira Morais (UFMA), Nayara Ferreira Santos (Baru Tech) and Romério Rodrigues dos Santos Silva (UFMA).

Scientific background

The foundation of all this work is chemometrics applied to simple measurements. For more than ten years I have developed fast, clean methods, both qualitative and quantitative, based on digital images, spectroscopy and chromatography, in matrices such as soils, seized drugs, sanitizers and beverages.
